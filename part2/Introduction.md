## 1. Introduction

**Courtsite** (`https://www.courtsite.my`), operated by Courtsite Web Solutions Sdn. Bhd. (2024b), is a Malaysian web-based software-as-a-service (SaaS) reservation and payment platform. Designed as a multi-sided business-to-business-to-consumer (B2B2C) marketplace, its core purpose is to connect individual players with independent local sports facilities—referred to as "Partner Centres"—to browse real-time availability, reserve court slots, and complete online payments (Courtsite Web Solutions Sdn. Bhd., 2024b, Clauses 2, 5).

The platform serves a dual target audience within the local sports ecosystem. On the consumer side, it caters to recreational athletes, casual players, and team organizers aged 18 and above seeking a streamlined alternative to manual phone or walk-in bookings (Courtsite Web Solutions Sdn. Bhd., 2024b, Clause 3). On the commercial side, it serves independent local venue operators—such as badminton, pickleball, and futsal centres—that rely on Courtsite's digital infrastructure to manage court inventory, scheduling, and upfront payment collection (Courtsite Web Solutions Sdn. Bhd., 2024b, Clauses 2, 5).

Within the broader technology landscape, Courtsite exemplifies the hyper-local digitization of everyday community infrastructure. Although significantly smaller in scale than global super-apps or e-commerce platforms, niche vertical SaaS systems like Courtsite increasingly act as primary digital gatekeepers to physical recreational spaces. As local sports complexes transition toward exclusive online reservations, the platform's software architecture, third-party data flows, and contractual terms directly govern consumer autonomy, data privacy, and financial fairness (Courtsite Web Solutions Sdn. Bhd., 2024a, 2024b).

Building on our preliminary investigation, this report conducts a comprehensive ethical evaluation of Courtsite. It begins by mapping the direct and indirect stakeholders affected by the system, followed by a structured analysis of key privacy, accountability, and fairness concerns under the ACM/IEEE-CS Software Engineering Code of Ethics (Association for Computing Machinery [ACM], 1999). Where clauses within the Code reach their limits or conflict (ACM, 1999), broader ethical frameworks—specifically Deontology and Utilitarianism—are applied to resolve the dilemmas, culminating in critical reflections and actionable technical and policy recommendations to improve the platform's ethical standing.
