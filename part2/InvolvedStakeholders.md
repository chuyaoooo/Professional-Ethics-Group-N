## 2. Involved Stakeholders

Because Courtsite operates as a multi-sided digital intermediary governing access to physical sports facilities, its software architecture and legal policies create cascading effects across five distinct stakeholder groups. Mapping both primary participants and indirect non-users reveals how the platform's design redistributes financial risk, operational burden, and privacy exposure across the local sports ecosystem.

### 2.1 Direct Users (Recreational Athletes and Team Organizers)

Direct users are individuals aged 18 and above who register accounts on Courtsite to browse venues, reserve slots, and complete upfront digital payments (Courtsite Web Solutions Sdn. Bhd., 2024b, Clauses 2–3). Four platform practices identified in our preliminary investigation affect them directly:

* **Non-Refundable Checkout and Absence of Dispute Workflows:** Once payment is completed, all bookings are treated as final and non-refundable unless the user can prove a "technical glitch at no fault of the User" (Courtsite Web Solutions Sdn. Bhd., 2024b, Clause 5). Because the web dashboard provides no self-service cancellation, modification, or dispute-resolution feature, users absorb full financial loss during unexpected emergencies, venue defects, or payment-gateway timeouts. Public user discussions on Malaysian community forums (e.g., Lowyat.net) corroborate this risk, documenting instances where e-wallet payment errors leave users uncertain whether funds were deducted after their selected court session vanished from the platform.
* **Bundled Consent and Data Coercion:** Although the Privacy Policy states that supplying personal data is "optional," withholding consent to any listed purpose—including direct marketing, benchmarking, and statistical profiling—results in total denial of booking services (Courtsite Web Solutions Sdn. Bhd., 2024a, Clauses 3, 5–6). Hands-on inspection of the registration flow confirms the absence of granular opt-out checkboxes for marketing, while browser developer tools inspection reveals third-party analytics and cookies firing prior to explicit user opt-in (see Figures X–Y).
* **Unilateral Contract Changes and Account Termination:** Users are automatically bound to policy amendments upon continued use without active notification, and their accounts may be suspended or terminated at any time without notice or financial compensation—even when holding paid future bookings (Courtsite Web Solutions Sdn. Bhd., 2024b, Clauses 1, 21).

### 2.2 Operating Company (Courtsite Web Solutions Sdn. Bhd.)

As the platform owner and commercial operator, Courtsite Web Solutions Sdn. Bhd. is directly affected by its own intermediary legal architecture and data-governance practices. By contractually framing itself as a passive technology facilitator that takes no part in booking agreements, the company minimizes warranty obligations, dispute-handling overhead, and direct liability for facility conditions (Courtsite Web Solutions Sdn. Bhd., 2024b, Clauses 1, 10–11, 13). This disclaimer is reinforced operationally on system-generated booking invoices, which reiterate that Courtsite accepts no obligations regarding service delivery between users and Partner Centres.

However, these practices introduce strategic and regulatory vulnerabilities for the company. Relying on broad cross-border data transfers, partner data sharing, and an August 2024 Privacy Policy that still classifies the firm under the legacy term "data user" exposes Courtsite to compliance risk under Malaysia's *Personal Data Protection (Amendment) Act 2024* (enforced across 2025), which reclassified organizations as "data controllers," imposed direct security duties on data processors, mandated Data Protection Officer (DPO) appointments, and introduced mandatory breach notifications (Courtsite Web Solutions Sdn. Bhd., 2024a, Clauses 1, 4; Personal Data Protection (Amendment) Act 2024). Over time, perceived unfairness in rigid refund rules also threatens user trust and drives repeat players back toward direct venue bookings.

### 2.3 Software Developers and Technical Staff

Courtsite's engineering team designs, deploys, and maintains the single-page web application, payment-gateway integrations, database schemas, and third-party tracking scripts. Developers are directly impacted by two intersecting practices:

* **Ethical Tension in Requirements Implementation:** Engineers are tasked with translating restrictive business rules—such as rigid non-refundable payment states, bundled clickwrap consent screens, and pre-consent analytics instrumentation—into executable code. This places them in direct tension between employer loyalty and professional duties to prioritize user welfare and privacy-by-design (Association for Computing Machinery [ACM], 1999, Clauses 1.02, 3.12).
* **Operational Accountability for "Technical Glitches":** Because Clause 5 ties user refund eligibility exclusively to proven "technical glitches," while Clause 12 disclaims liability for internet delays or faulty devices (Courtsite Web Solutions Sdn. Bhd., 2024b, Clauses 5, 12), developers bear indirect pressure whenever asynchronous payment callbacks (e.g., FPX or Touch 'n Go eWallet redirects) fail or desynchronize. Without robust transaction idempotency, automated rollback states, or auditable error logs, engineers leave both users and customer-support teams unable to verify whether a failed booking was caused by platform code or external network latency.

### 2.4 Business Partners and Venue Workers (Partner Centres and Front-Desk Staff)

Partner Centres—comprising over 200 independent Malaysian badminton, pickleball, and futsal venues—and their front-desk employees experience a sharp trade-off from Courtsite's design:

* **Revenue Protection vs. Platform Dependency:** Automated upfront payment collection (Courtsite Web Solutions Sdn. Bhd., 2024b, Clause 5) significantly reduces last-minute no-shows compared to manual WhatsApp bookings, stabilizing venue cash flow. Yet operating within Courtsite's marketplace subjects venues to recurring per-booking transaction fees while ceding primary control over the digital booking interface.
* **Absorbing Offloaded Disputes and Liability:** Under Terms of Use Clauses 1, 5, 6, and 13, Partner Centres bear 100% of legal warranties, rescheduling decisions, and user complaints ("Any complaints between Partner Centres and Users must be taken up with each other directly") (Courtsite Web Solutions Sdn. Bhd., 2024b, Clause 6). Consequently, front-desk staff—who have zero control over Courtsite's payment gateway or refund policy—must absorb face-to-face conflict from frustrated players whenever digital checkout errors or emergency cancellations occur.
* **Downstream Data-Custodianship Burden:** Courtsite shares user personal data with Partner Centres for "user or customer management procedures" (Courtsite Web Solutions Sdn. Bhd., 2024a, Clause 3). Many small local sports halls lack dedicated IT security personnel or formal data-retention protocols, exposing both venue owners and workers to accidental data leakage or PDPA compliance failures when handling exported customer records.

### 2.5 Indirect Stakeholders (Non-Users Affected by the System)

Two groups who never directly create accounts on Courtsite are nevertheless materially affected by its practices:

* **Invited Teammates, Accompanying Players, and Minors:** Because sports such as badminton and futsal are played in groups, one team organizer or parent frequently books courts on behalf of multiple players—including minors under 18 who are legally barred from registering accounts (Courtsite Web Solutions Sdn. Bhd., 2024b, Clause 3). Under Privacy Policy Clause 1, whenever an organizer discloses another person's details to Courtsite, the platform states it will "assume, without independent verification, that you have obtained such individual's consent" (Courtsite Web Solutions Sdn. Bhd., 2024a, Clause 1). Simultaneously, Terms of Use Clause 13 disclaims Courtsite's liability for any loss, damage, or injury caused "to any person for whom you have made the Booking for" (Courtsite Web Solutions Sdn. Bhd., 2024b, Clause 13). As a result, non-users and youth players have their personal data processed and legal recourse waived without ever viewing or agreeing to the platform's terms.
* **Digitally Excluded Community Members:** As neighbourhood sports complexes transition exclusively to Courtsite to eliminate manual desk management, older residents, low-digital-literacy individuals, and unbanked or cash-reliant players lose practical access to local recreational infrastructure. Our platform walkthrough confirms that reserving a slot requires online account creation, acceptance of complex English or Malay legal text, and cashless digital payment—offering no cash-on-arrival or offline reservation pathway. Even without visiting `courtsite.my`, these community members experience structural exclusion from public-facing recreational spaces.
